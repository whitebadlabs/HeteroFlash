## 1. Mechanism

- **Backbone.** The backbone is a DFlash-style parallel block drafter: 5 layers, hidden size 4096, and a block of 1 anchor plus 15 candidates (16 positions) [A, paper]. SGLang's `--speculative-num-draft-tokens 16` counts the anchor. vLLM's `num_speculative_tokens=15` does not. Both describe the same block.
- **Target features.** Features from target layers {1, 9, 17, 25, 33} of Qwen3-8B (36 layers) are fused and fed as K/V context to every drafter layer [A, paper]. In the vLLM PR test fixture the input lists `aux_hidden_state_layer_ids` [2, 10, 18, 26, 34], and the assertion expects `target_layer_ids` [1, 9, 17, 25, 33]. The PR docs say `selected_prefix_feature_layers` is 1-based and must include the last layer (PR diff). Which convention vLLM uses when it captures these layers has to be checked against `qwen3_dflash.py` before wiring GLM.
- **Attention.** Attention is block-causal in every layer: position t sees the target context plus block positions 0..t. DFlash uses bidirectional attention inside the block [A, paper]. The PR rejects any non-causal draft layer.
- **Adjacent causal injection.** This is the new part. After each drafter layer ℓ, position t takes a gated message from position t−1's "predicted feature" z = √r·normalize(R_ℓ u):
  - g = σ(G_q z_t + G_p z_{t−1} + b)
  - h = u + RMS(u)·W_ℓ(g ⊙ A z_{t−1})

  The messages use pre-update features, so all positions still update in parallel. Conditioning on the predecessor's guess deepens by one hop per layer. The feature space is a frozen PCA-whitened projection of the target LM-head rows (r=1024, message dim 512). Injection adds about 33M parameters, roughly 3% of the drafter [A, paper].
- **Predecessor-conditioned decoding.** After the last layer, top-K=16 candidates per position are taken. A K²-pair transition table is scored in parallel and walked left to right [A, paper]. The vLLM PR implements this as a fp32 refinement table plus a candidate walk.
- **Size and cost.** The Qwen3-8B drafter is about 1B params BF16 and the Qwen3.8-27B drafter about 2B (HF cards [A]). Both share the target's embedding and LM head. On Qwen3-8B the DSpine round takes 7.14 ms versus DFlash2's 7.19 ms, and injection adds at most 0.15 ms per round. Verification is 72–76% of round time [A, paper; GPU not named]. That implies drafting plus overhead of about 1.7–2.0 ms per round on their GPU [E].
- **How it differs from DFlash2.** DFlash2 is also a parallel block drafter with a candidate walk; the vLLM `DSpineSpeculator` subclasses `DFlash2Speculator`. DSpine adds three things: causal in-block attention, the per-layer predecessor injection, and the pairwise last-write refinement. The ablation drops τ 6.6–7.1% without refinement and 8.8–10.7% without refinement and injection [A, paper].

## 2. Training

- **Paper setup.** The paper trained on ShareGPT prompts with responses regenerated by the target, thinking disabled. Training ran 6 epochs, 3,655 steps, global batch 112, max sequence length 3,072, 512 anchors per sequence, with AdamW at a peak LR of 6e-4 and cosine decay [A, paper].
- **Loss.** The loss combines:
  - a distillation term (−α log q + (1−α)·L1(q, p), α=0.1);
  - a layer-wise cosine loss to the frozen code table, with position decay γ=7;
  - a correct-predecessor curriculum.

  Total tokens, GPU type and GPU-hours are **not reported** [A, paper].
- **Model-card setup.** The repo and model-card tables use a different setup: DSpine, DSpark and DFlash2 were all retrained on Open-PerfectBlend under one budget [A, repo]. Do not mix these numbers with the paper's.
- **Targets trained.** Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B (paper), and Qwen3.8-27B (card). All are dense; the 27B is a hybrid with linear attention. **No MoE target.**
- **Training code.** It is in SpecForge, not AngelSpec. PR #965 ("independently registered draft training algorithm") was merged on 27 Oct 2026 and was validated on H20. PR #1038 (loss-normalization fix plus a Qwen3-8B online example) is still open. The HF card's line "training code not yet released" is out of date. AngelSpec (arXiv 2607.25852) is a separate Tencent framework (MTP, DFly on Hy3) and is not where DSpine lives.

## 3. Evaluation

### Paper [A]

- **Settings.** GSM8K, MATH-500, HumanEval, MBPP, LiveCodeBench, MT-Bench and Arena-Hard. Thinking off, T=0 and T=1, max 2,048 new tokens, 15 draft tokens. SGLang with up to 16 concurrent requests per GPU.
- **Mean τ (Qwen3-8B), T0 / T1.** DFlash 3.77 / 3.39, DFlash2 4.39 / 3.90, DSpine 4.82 / 4.42.
- **Per domain (Qwen3-8B, T1).**

| Method | GSM8K | MATH-500 | MT-Bench | Arena-Hard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DSpine | 5.31 | 5.05 | 3.79 | 3.62 |
| DFlash2 | 4.56 | 4.47 | 3.35 | 3.29 |

  On chat at T1, DSpine leads DFlash2 by **+13% (MT-Bench) and +10% (Arena-Hard)**.
- **Throughput.** At T0 and concurrency 2–32: +11.1% over DFlash2 on Qwen3-8B, +6.5% on 4B, and +23.3% over DFlash. The peak is 4.01× over autoregressive decoding (MATH-500, concurrency 2). The GPU is not named.

### Repo / HF table [A], different training data

The Qwen3-8B T0 average is 7.83 for DSpine versus 7.55 for DFlash2 (+3.7%). MT-Bench is 5.63 vs 5.40. The sources do not explain the roughly 1.6× gap to the paper's 4.82.

### Qwen3.8-27B card [A]

This is the closest analogue to GLM: a hybrid linear-attention target.

- **Settings.** DSpine uses block 16 and DFlash2 block 8. Thinking at xhigh. SGLang 0.5.22 at concurrency 8.
- **MT-Bench τ (DSpine vs DFlash2).**
  - T0: 4.48 vs 4.11 (**+9%**)
  - T1: 4.03 vs 3.89 (**+3.6%**)
- **Math (GSM8K τ, DSpine vs DFlash2).**
  - T0: 7.20 vs 5.69 (+27%)
  - T1: 6.31 vs 5.45 (+16%)
- **Agent benchmarks.** On WorkBuddy, SWE-bench Pro and τ² the gain is +15–33%, and end-to-end throughput rises 8–21% (e.g. WorkBuddy Office low: 805.5 vs 664.8 tok/s).
- **Hardware.** Not stated.
- **Caveat.** These τ values were measured with reasoning at xhigh, so they include thinking tokens, which are easier to accept than final prose. Plain-prose ratios are likely lower.

### vLLM PR author [3P]

On one L20D at batch 1, greedy, 256 tokens: DSpine 257.8 tok/s (τ 3.38) versus DFlash 167.8 tok/s (τ 2.44) and no speculation at 97.7 tok/s. There was no DFlash2 comparison.

## 4. vLLM integration (PR #63704)

- **Status.** **Open, not merged.** It adds 2,618 lines across 15 files. benchislett requested changes on 9 Nov: move the config logic to a subclass, turn the k mismatch into a warning, and trim the tests. Overall verdict: "looks good."
- **Structure.**
  - `qwen3_dspine.py` (`DSpineQwen3ForCausalLM` subclasses `DFlashQwen3ForCausalLM`).
  - `dspine/speculator.py` (subclasses `DFlash2Speculator`, with a Triton fused bridge).
  - Config routing: `method=dspine` is mapped to `dflash`.
- **KV cache.** The drafter keeps **its own full-attention KV cache layers**: `get_draft_kv_cache_layer_names()` returns 5 layers. The attention is the ordinary DFlash backend with `causal=True` in all 5 layers (`get_draft_attn_causal() == [True]*5`).
- **Hidden-state capture.** It reuses the DFlash aux-hidden-state path.
- **Constraints.** Model Runner V2 only. TP=1. FP4 KV caches (nvfp4, fp4_mx_block16) are rejected. The target LM head must be an unquantized, row-contiguous BF16/FP32 tensor. `num_speculative_tokens` must equal block−1, as a hard error today.
- **Would causal attention avoid the GLM MLA C4 page-stride failure?** **No** [E, from the code]. Causality changes the mask, not the cache layout. DSpine still adds a separate GQA drafter KV group, which is exactly what broke "GLM MLA parent-page stride must be an exact number of C4 pages". It needs the same fix as DFlash2: a drafter KV group sharing or aligned to the MLA tensors (the working 2×Spark trick), or drafter page padding to a C4 multiple.
- **Work needed on eugr/spark-vllm-b12x [E].**
  1. Confirm the fork has MRv2 and `DFlash2Speculator`, not only DFlash. I could not check this from here.
  2. Rebase the 15 files onto the fork.
  3. Fix the KV-group alignment.
  4. Wire GLM aux capture for 5 layers across the KDA and MLA mix.
  5. Keep the LM head in BF16. That is an extra read per draft if the target head is quantized.
  6. Account for the KDA recurrent-state rollback. It scales with the drafted length: about 150 MB per drafted token on the 27B hybrid, so +1.2 GB for 16 vs 8 [3P, GGUF card].

## 5. GLM-5.3-Flash prose estimate [E]

**Expected accept length.** Our DFlash2-G prose acceptance is assumed at 3.0–3.7. Measured DSpine/DFlash2 chat ratios are 1.036–1.09 (27B card) and 1.10–1.13 (8B paper, T1). That gives DSpine prose τ of about **3.1–4.2, central ~3.5**. The "prose penalty" is visible in both sources:
- 27B MT-Bench T1 is 4.03 versus GSM8K 6.31.
- The llama.cpp port measured 2.5 tokens per verify on story prose.

**Unions.** U is linearly interpolated: U(4)=22.7, U(8)=38, **U(16)=56.3**. A log-t sensitivity gives U(16)=65.5. Expert phase = U × 42 × 11 MB / 380 GB/s = 1.216 ms per unit of U, giving 27.6, 46.2 and 68.5 ms (79.6 ms with the log-t union).

**Drafter costs [E].** These assume weight reads at 210 GB/s:
- MTP-3: 6 ms (3 sequential 1-layer passes).
- DFlash2-G: 16 ms (1.7B BF16 = 3.4 GB).
- DSpine: 12 ms (5 layers × 4096, about 1.2B, plus 0.15 ms injection). This could reach about 19 ms if sized like the 2B 27B drafter.

These costs leave out the shared target LM-head read per draft step. That read is roughly the same for both drafters, so the break-even ratio barely moves, but every absolute tok/s figure below is optimistic. DSpine also requires that head in BF16, while a DFlash2 path could use an FP8 head.

| Config | Fixed 56 ms (FP8): cycle | Fixed 56 ms (FP8): tok/s | Fixed 38 ms (NVFP4): cycle | Fixed 38 ms (NVFP4): tok/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MTP-3, τ 2.2 | 93.6 | **23.5** | 75.6 | **29.1** |
| DFlash2 k7, τ 3.0 / 3.3 / 3.7 | 122.2 | 24.5 / 27.0 / 30.3 | 104.2 | 28.8 / 31.7 / 35.5 |
| DSpine k15, τ 3.0 / 3.5 / 4.0 / 4.5 / 5.0 | 140.5 | 21.4 / 24.9 / 28.5 / 32.0 / 35.6 | 122.5 | 24.5 / 28.6 / 32.7 / 36.7 / 40.8 |

**Break-even.** DSpine at k=15 needs τ_DSpine ≥ **1.150× τ_DFlash2** (FP8) or **1.176×** (NVFP4). With the log-t union the bar is 1.24× / 1.28×.
- At DFlash2 τ 3.0–3.7, DSpine needs **3.45–4.25** (FP8) or **3.53–4.35** (NVFP4).
- The measured chat ratios (1.04–1.13) fall **below** this bar.
- Expectation: **on prose, DSpine at k=15 ties or loses to DFlash2.** It beats MTP-3 only if τ ≥ about 3.3 (FP8) or 3.6 (NVFP4).

**DSpine truncated to k=7.** This is allowed in llama.cpp but is a hard error in the vLLM PR. The union matches DFlash2 (cycle 118.2 / 100.2 ms) and the drafter is cheaper. With τ ≈ 1.05–1.10× DFlash2 and a cycle ratio of 1.034, it would be about 9–14% faster than DFlash2, or about 6–12% after allowing a little acceptance lost to truncation. That is a modest, plausible win.

**Can anything reach 80 tok/s?**
- The FP8 fixed path plus 4 ms alone needs τ ≥ 4.8 (NVFP4: 3.4).
- At U(16) it needs **τ ≥ 11.2 (FP8) or 9.8 (NVFP4)**.
- No prose number anywhere comes close (the best chat τ is 5.6 in the README table). **80 tok/s on prose is not reachable.** Even code-level τ of about 7.5 gives 53 / 61 tok/s.

## 6. Cost to get a GLM-5.3-Flash DSpine drafter [E]

- **Data.** The paper's run implies about 68k sequences (3,655 × 112 / 6) of at most 3,072 tokens. That is roughly 40–100M regenerated tokens, upper bound 210M.
  - On our Spark at batch 1 (about 25 tok/s) that would take months, which is infeasible.
  - Batched on Spark, at maybe 300–600 tok/s aggregate, it would take about 1–4 days.
  - On a rented 8×H200 node with SGLang FP8, about 3–10 h.
  - Better: use our own prose and agent prompts with thinking matched to our use.
- **Training.**
  - **Online (SpecForge).** The 310B target must stay resident, so this needs an 8×H100/H200-class node.
  - **Offline hidden-state dumps.** 5 layers × hidden (~5k) × 2 B ≈ 50 KB per token, about 5 TB per 100M tokens.
  - **Compute.** The drafter needs 6 × 1.2B × ~3.4B draft positions ≈ 2.4e19 FLOPs. The target forward needs a similar order. That is about 10–30 node-hours including overhead, roughly $0.5–2k rented. The paper gives no GPU-hours to check against.
- **Integration.** Port the PR, fix KV alignment, wire GLM aux capture, and allow k<15. That is about 1–2 engineer-weeks plus validation.
- **The single result that would justify it.** On Spark, run the released **Qwen3.8-27B DSpine vs DFlash2** on our prose set at T=1. Measure the τ ratio at k=15 and at k=7, plus P(accept ≥ 8).
  - Spend only if the ratio is ≥ about **1.15–1.2 at k=15** (or ≥ about 1.1 at k=7) on prose.
  - Or justify it on the agent workload, where the 27B card already shows +15–33% τ. That is above the k=15 break-even, so DSpine is the better bet for agents than for prose.
